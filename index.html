Swara Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 October 2026

Swara shows which nostril is flowing, which element is rising, and when each changes, worked out on your own device from the sun and moon. It comes as an app for Wear OS watches and an app for Android phones. This policy explains what the apps do with your information. The short version: they use your approximate location to work out sunrise and sunset, and keep everything on your own devices. Swara has no internet permission and sends nothing to us or anyone else. The only things that move are your own HRV readings and settings between your watch and the phone it is paired with, and, if your phone's backup is on, an encrypted copy of the phone app's data to your own Google account.

Nothing else is shared between them.

That is everything. Swara does not ask for your name, email address or any account. It does not use your contacts, photos, or camera. The only body sensor it uses is the watch's heart sensor, and only while you run an HRV reading (see below). The phone app plays soft cue sounds and vibrates for its breathing timer, which needs the vibration permission; that reads nothing from you.

Microphone

The Bhramari tab can show the pitch of your humming as you practise. This needs the microphone permission, which the app only asks for when you open that tab and choose to use it.

  • The microphone runs only while you have pressed Start in that tab, and stops the moment you press Stop, leave the tab, or tap the notification described below.
  • Unlike the rest of Swara, a Bhramari session is designed to keep working with the screen off, so you can lock the phone mid-practice. Android only allows a microphone to keep running once the screen is off from behind what it calls a foreground service, and it requires that service to show a notification for as long as it runs — that is Android's rule, not a choice made here, and it means you can always see, and stop, a session that is still listening. This needs two more permissions Android defines for exactly this purpose (to run that service, and to show that notification); neither reads anything from you beyond what the microphone permission already covers.
  • Audio is turned into a single number (its pitch, in Hz) on the phone and discarded immediately. No audio is ever recorded to a file, kept after that instant, or sent anywhere.
  • If you decline the permission, the rest of Bhramari still works: you can read about the candidate frequencies, just without seeing your own pitch.

Heart sensor and HRV readings

The watch app can take a short resting reading of heart-rate variability (HRV). You can start one on the watch (Measure HRV), or from the phone app (the Nadi Shodhana and Bhramari tabs, or Journal > HRV). A reading needs the body sensors permission on the watch, which the app asks for when you first measure on the watch.

  • The heart sensor runs only while a reading is in progress, and stops when it finishes, when you stop or cancel it, or (for a reading started on the watch) when you leave the screen.
  • The watch's heart sensor reports the time between each heartbeat. Swara uses those timings to calculate the reading, then discards them. It saves only the result: the HRV figure, the average heart rate, the time, a quality grade, and the label you picked.
  • The sensor is reached through Samsung's Health Sensor Service, which is part of your watch. Swara itself sends none of this to Samsung, or to anyone else, and has no internet permission.
  • A reading started from the phone wakes the watch and holds its screen on while it runs, so the reading is not interrupted. Android requires an app that reads the heart sensor this way to run as a visible foreground service, so the watch shows a notification and a counter screen until the reading ends; the screen is released, and the notification removed, as soon as it does. This needs the permissions Android defines for exactly this (a health foreground service, and keeping the screen awake). Neither reads anything from you.
  • Between your watch and phone. Readings you save on the watch are sent to the phone paired with it, where the phone app keeps them so you can see your HRV over weeks and months. While a phone-started reading runs, the watch also sends the phone its progress (how many clean beats so far, and your heart rate) and the phone sends the watch "start", "stop" and "cancel". All of this travels through Google Play services' Wearable Data Layer, only between a watch and the phone it is paired with, and only to the copy of Swara installed on each. It is not something Swara sends over the internet. Depending on how your devices are connected at the time, Google Play services may carry it over Bluetooth or over Google's network; that transfer is Google's, under its own privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy), and Swara has no control over how it is routed.
  • Live heart rate during practice is off unless you switch it on, and the app warns about battery use first. While a practice runs, the watch keeps its heart sensor on, shows a notification, and sends your heart rate to the paired phone every couple of seconds over the same Wearable Data Layer. It stops when the practice stops, if the phone stops asking for a minute, or after an hour. The phone keeps the individual readings only in memory and saves just a summary with the practice.
  • You can delete the readings by clearing Swara's storage or uninstalling it. The watch and the phone each keep their own copy, so clearing one does not clear the other.
  • These are personal readings from a wrist sensor, which approximates HRV and is less reliable if you move. They are not a medical measurement.

Location

Swara asks for the approximate location permission only. It does not ask for precise location, and it does not use the separate "all the time" background location permission.

  • The apps read your location when you open them, and (on the watch) when the tile or complication finds that its stored location is more than six hours old. They only ever ask for a single reading, never a continuous stream, and never track where you have been.
  • Your location is read through Google Play services, which is how Android and Wear OS apps get a position. Google handles that request under its own privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). Swara receives the result and does nothing with it beyond the sunrise and sunset calculation.
  • If you decline the permission, Swara still works. It shows times for a place guessed from your time zone (or a default) until you allow location or choose a city or coordinates yourself, and it says so on screen.

Where your information goes

Nowhere that we or anyone else receives it. Swara does not have permission to use the internet, so it cannot send anything off your watch or phone. All calculations, including the position of the sun and moon, happen on the device itself. The one movement of data is your HRV readings and the commands described above, between your own watch and the phone paired with it, carried by Google Play services.

Links and pronunciation. Some pages (Discover, Learn and Bhramari) have links to a study or a source text. Tapping one opens it in your phone's own browser: that is your browser making its own request, separate from Swara. The 🔊 buttons that say Sanskrit words use your phone's own text-to-speech voice, offline; the words are not sent anywhere.

Alerts and reminders. If you switch on swara change alerts or plan reminders, they are scheduled by your phone or watch's own alarm service and shown as notifications. Nothing leaves the device to arrange them. They need the notification permission, and on the phone, optionally, the permission to time them to the minute.

Swara contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking software. We do not sell, rent or share your information, because we never receive it.

Backup to your own Google account (phone app). So your journal is not lost when you change phones, the phone app lets Android's own backup service include its settings, plan, practice journal, HRV readings and stored location. This is Android's feature, not Swara's: the copy goes to your own Google account, is encrypted (on recent Android versions, end-to-end with your screen lock), and is restored only to your own devices. Swara itself still sends nothing, and we cannot see it. You control it in your phone's Settings, under Google and Backup; switching it off there stops it. Test logs kept by test builds are never included. The watch app does not use backup.

Exporting your data (phone app). From the Journal tab you can save a copy of your data as a file, or your practices and readings as a spreadsheet (CSV), to a place you choose, and later load the copy back. Where the file goes after that is up to you.

How long it is kept

The stored location is replaced each time the app takes a fresh reading or you choose another place. The location, the HRV readings, and the phone app's practice journal, plan and settings are kept until you clear the app's storage or uninstall Swara. The journal keeps your latest 2,000 practices, the watch keeps your latest 60 HRV readings, and the phone keeps up to 5,000. A backup in your Google account is kept and deleted under Google's rules for Android backups.

Your choices

  • Turn location off at any time in your watch's or phone's Settings, under Apps and permissions. The stored location stays on the device until you clear it, but is no longer updated. On the phone you can also pick a city or enter coordinates yourself.
  • Delete everything by clearing Swara's storage in Settings, or by uninstalling the app. The phone app can delete a single practice from the journal (tap it), but not yet a single HRV reading. To remove the backup copy too, turn off backup for Swara or delete the backup in your Google account. Because we hold no copy, there is nothing further to request from us.
  • Turn off backup in your phone's Settings, under Google and Backup.

Children

Swara is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, children included.

Not health or medical advice

Swara reports a traditional practice. It does not diagnose, treat or monitor any condition. The one piece of health-related information it handles is the HRV reading you choose to take, which stays on your watch and its paired phone and is described above. The practice journal in the phone app is a private record of your own; it stays on your phone and in your own backup.
